One system.

Orbit, Tally, and Verde are working React prototypes built to test one idea: a coherent design system should create consistency without erasing each product’s character.

01 / The system

Shared foundations.
Distinct product voices.

The prototypes share component behavior, typography, spacing, radii, and interaction patterns. Product identity comes from controlled theme overrides: color, emphasis, and content hierarchy change while the underlying system stays stable.

  1. 01
    Token first

    Color, spacing, type, radius, and shadows come from one JSON source.

  2. 02
    One clear emphasis

    Each screen uses a single highlight to make the next action obvious.

  3. 03
    Theme parity

    Light and dark modes preserve hierarchy instead of simply inverting colors.

  4. 04
    Accessible states

    Status and progress use labels and values—not color alone.

02 / Orbit · Web dashboard

A weekly health check for product teams.

Orbit brings delivery signals into one scan: shipped work, cycle time, bugs, sprint progress, velocity, and active-project risk. Its bento layout gives product and engineering leads a status view without reconstructing it from tickets and spreadsheets.

  • KPI cards with deltas and sparklines
  • Weekly velocity with selectable time ranges
  • Sprint progress and risk-aware project filtering
  • Visible keyboard search and focus states

03 / Tally · Mobile finance

Money decisions without spreadsheet density.

Tally is organized around two recurring questions: “what happened?” and “am I on track?” Large figures, aligned amounts, category meters, and plainly worded warnings keep the answer visible without turning the app into a wall of charts.

  • Balance, change, and recent activity at a glance
  • Monthly budget ring with days remaining
  • Category progress with explicit over-budget labels
  • A focused savings goal with one clear action

04 / Verde · Mobile utility

Plant care reduced to what matters today.

Verde turns different watering, light, and humidity needs into a short daily round. The interface favors direct instructions over encyclopedic content, with primary actions kept within easy reach and plant status always stated in words.

  • A daily watering round with visible completion
  • Room filters for larger plant collections
  • Care needs summarized through three compact measures
  • One-tap watering logs and a reserved identification flow

Prototype boundary

The interface layer is implemented. The production services are a direction.

The screens are real React code, not static mock-ups. Proposed integrations—such as Jira and GitHub for Orbit, bank connections for Tally, or plant identification and sync for Verde—are documented production paths, not features claimed as complete.
